# Jev lane-pick backtest — 2026-10-09 (reading of the numbers)

Tables: `jev-backtest.md` next to this file, regenerated by `python3 scripts/jev_backtest.py`
(199 past tasks; 173 answered; 26 no pick, almost all refused by the PII scrub because the
spec held an email or phone-shaped string; one Jev call per task, ~3-4k input tokens, ~0.25 s;
the whole replay cost under $0.05 at TypeSafe's $0.042/M).

## What Jev did

- **It picked `codex-sol` (gpt-5.6-sol, default effort) 110 of 173 times** (mean confidence
  0.82). That lane is also the best-evidenced one in the log: code-feature 85% first-try (n=26),
  code-fix 86% (n=14). Agreement with what actually ran is low (32%) mostly because the actual
  runs were spread over effort flags and the claude engine, not because Jev was wrong.
- **It never picked `codex-sol-medium`** after its criteria were narrowed to small bounded
  fixes, and it picked `codex-astra` once (confidence 0.32). The 9 tasks that really ran on
  astra (8/8 first-try) were all sent to `codex-sol` by Jev. Whether sol would have passed them
  is unknown; the estimate assumes sol's 85% average.
- **It sent 20 claude-sonnet tasks to `claude-haiku`** and 25 to `codex-sol`. The sonnet rows are
  the research and code-feature work that failed on the Write-only engine (34% and 57% first-try).
  Moving them to codex is probably right for the ones that needed a shell; moving to haiku is
  right only for pure file writes.
- **format-conversion and probe tasks all went to `claude-haiku`**; the real lane was gemini-flash
  or codex. No baseline cell exists for haiku yet (2 tasks today), so those estimates are
  "unknown", not wins.

## The estimate, and why to distrust its size

| | Actual | Under Jev's picks |
|---|---|---|
| First-try pass (known cells, n=133) | 64.8% | 76.8% |
| Worker tokens, sum of deltas | — | −3.08M (−28% of the 11.2M logged) |

Most of the token delta comes from one place: the 25 tasks that ran with a blank model or at high
effort during the broken-check runs (145k tokens median, 33-40% first-try) are replaced in the
estimate by codex-sol's 50k median and 85% first-try. The check preflight shipped in this PR removes
that waste without any model change; Jev gets credit for it here only because the backtest cannot
tell a bad model choice from a bad check. Read the first-try gain as "Jev agrees with the log's
best lane", not as a measured improvement.

Known cells only: docs (2 of 8 known), research (15 of 33), format-conversion and probe (0) are
too thin to say anything. code-feature (83 known) and code-fix (23) carry the result.

## What to change before trusting the picker

1. Add a second question or sharper criteria so Jev separates "needs a shell and tests" from
   "pure file write". Today the claude lanes are chosen for 50 tasks on wording alone, and the
   claude engine has a ~45k-token floor per task (Claude Code's own prompt), so haiku is not
   automatically cheaper than codex for small work.
2. Keep the medium and high codex lanes only if the shadow data shows Jev using them; otherwise
   collapse to codex-sol and codex-astra and let effort be a spec decision.
3. Re-run this backtest after two weeks of shadow rows (every real run now logs `jev_pick`), when
   the per-lane cells for haiku and astra have n ≥ 3. That is the step-6 task in the brain.
